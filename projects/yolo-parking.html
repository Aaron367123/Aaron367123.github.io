Parking Detection with YOLOv8

An object-detection model that looks at an aerial photo of a parking lot and marks every space as free or taken. I trained YOLOv8 on a labelled dataset in Google Colab, measured how accurate it was, and tested it on new images it had never seen.

01 Overview

Finding a free spot in a big lot wastes time and fuel. A camera above the lot plus an object detector can count free spaces automatically and feed a sign or an app. This project was my first hands-on run through the full computer-vision workflow:

DatasetLabelled aerial images from Roboflow
TrainYOLOv8, 25 epochs on a T4 GPU
ValidatePrecision, recall, mAP, confusion matrix
PredictRun on unseen lot images

02 The dataset

I started from the open Parking Lot Availability dataset on Roboflow Universe and copied it into my own Roboflow project. This version has 135 images and two classes: empty spaces and spaces with a car in them. Roboflow handled the train/validation/test split and exported the data in YOLOv8 format.

03 Training

I trained in Google Colab on a free Tesla T4 GPU, using Roboflow's "train YOLOv8 on a custom dataset" notebook. The notebook fine-tunes a model that was already trained on a general dataset (transfer learning), which is why such a small dataset could still reach good accuracy.

Software: Ultralytics 8.0.196, PyTorch 2.2.1 with CUDA 12.1, Python 3.10. The trained network has 168 layers, 11.1 M parameters and 28.4 GFLOPs, which matches the small (s) YOLOv8 model.

For comparison, Roboflow's own hosted "Fast" model on the same data scored 76.2% mAP, so the YOLOv8 model I trained did noticeably better.

mAP@50 is high but mAP@50-95 is much lower. The model finds the spaces reliably, but its boxes don't line up tightly with the edges. That's acceptable for counting free spaces, but a bigger dataset would be needed for precise outlines.

05 Testing on new images

The real test is a lot the model has never seen. I ran it on new, unlabelled aerial photos and recorded a short demo video for the presentation.

06 Takeaways

  • Transfer learning makes small datasets workable: 135 images and about six minutes of GPU time gave almost 90% mAP@50.
  • How to read detection metrics: what precision vs. recall means for a parking app, and why mAP@50 and mAP@50-95 tell different stories.
  • Data quality matters more than settings. The class names exported from the dataset were confusing, and cleaning up labels is the first thing I'd do next.
  • Next steps: more images (night, rain, different camera angles), a larger YOLOv8 model, and a live count of free spaces from a video feed.
