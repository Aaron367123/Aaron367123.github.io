NBA Rookie Data Analysis

How has the NBA changed over 35 years? I loaded rookie statistics for every draft class from 1980 to 2015 into Metabase, asked four questions with SQL, and built a dashboard that shows the rise of the three-point shot.

03 Questions & findings

1 · Do rookies who play more minutes score more?

Yes, and clearly. Average points rise steadily with average minutes, and the spread widens at the top: rookies playing 30+ minutes range from solid starters to stars scoring 25+ points a game.

2 · How has three-point shooting changed?

This is the biggest trend in the data. Rookies in the early 1980s hardly took threes. By the end of the dataset, three-pointers have gone from a rarity to a normal part of every rookie's game.

3 · Are some draft classes better than others?

Average rookie efficiency by draft year is fairly flat, but it peaks in classes with standout rookies, for example the early-1990s class that included Shaquille O'Neal, and the classes of Kevin Durant (2007) and Devin Booker (2015).

4 · Shot selection: field goals vs. threes

Putting average field-goal attempts next to average three-point attempts shows the change in shot selection: overall attempts drift down while threes take up a growing share.

04 The SQL

I built each question in Metabase's visual query builder, then read the SQL it generated to check it was asking what I meant. This one is behind the shot-selection chart: two averages, grouped and ordered by draft year.

Metabase · FGA vs 3PA by draft year
SELECT
  "source"."year_drafted" AS "year_drafted",
  AVG("source"."fga") AS "avg",
  AVG("source"."3pa") AS "avg_2"
FROM
  (
    SELECT ... FROM
      "PUBLIC"."nba_rookies_by_year_20240415064734"
  ) AS "source"
GROUP BY
  "source"."year_drafted"
ORDER BY
  "source"."year_drafted" ASC

The inner SELECT lists every column and is shortened to ... here.

05 Reflections

  • Check the chart against the query. Re-reading the generated SQL showed that two chart titles didn't match the measures being plotted. Titles are part of the analysis and need checking too.
  • Averages hide things. One standout rookie can lift a whole draft year's efficiency. Medians, or weighting by games played, would make fairer comparisons.
  • Next steps: normalise for pace and minutes (per-36 stats), add three-point percentage next to volume, and compare rookies with the league as a whole.
